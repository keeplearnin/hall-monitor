## Controls

### HM-01

**Agent registry and approvals** (built)

Every agent has a typed manifest naming its owner (a person listed in registry/owners.yaml, never a team), risk tier, status, model, tools and data classes. Unregistered, inactive or unapproved agents are refused at run time: the manifest digest and the system-prompt template hash must match registry/approvals.yaml, and the template hash is recorded on every call. CI fails on an unapproved change.

- **Tests:** [`tests/test_registry.py`](../tests/test_registry.py) `test_unregistered_agent_is_refused`; [`tests/test_registry.py`](../tests/test_registry.py) `test_unapproved_manifest_is_refused`; [`tests/test_registry.py`](../tests/test_registry.py) `test_approvals_match_the_current_manifests_and_prompts`; [`tests/test_registry.py`](../tests/test_registry.py) `test_unapproved_prompt_is_refused_at_run_time`; [`tests/test_registry.py`](../tests/test_registry.py) `test_the_approved_prompt_covers_the_untrusted_claim_notice`
- **NIST AI RMF:** GOVERN 1.6 (Mechanisms are in place to inventory AI systems and are resourced according to organizational risk priorities); GOVERN 2.1 (Roles and responsibilities and lines of communication related to mapping, measuring, and managing AI risks are documented and are clear to individuals and teams throughout the organization); MAP 1.1 (Intended purposes, potentially beneficial uses, context-specific laws, norms and expectations, and prospective settings in which the AI system will be deployed are understood and documented); MANAGE 2.4 (Mechanisms are in place and applied, and responsibilities are assigned and understood, to supersede, disengage, or deactivate AI systems that demonstrate performance or outcomes inconsistent with intended use)
- **FINOS risks:** RI-05 Foundation Model Versioning; RI-18 Model Overreach / Expanded Use
- **FINOS mitigations:** MI-10 AI Model Version Pinning
- **Gaps:** Anyone who can commit can regenerate approvals.yaml. Review of that change depends on repository settings: .github/CODEOWNERS names an owner, but main has no branch protection yet, so it isn't enforced. Approvals pin each system-prompt template (the untrusted-claim notice included since review 6), but not the policy wording filled into it: an edit to the wording file changes what the coverage and critic agents are sent without a new approval. Nor do they pin the output schema the model is given, so a change to an output model (a new field constraint, say) needs no new approval; the API passes pydantic patterns to the model as description text, not as constraints, and they are checked only once the answer is back. Nor do they pin how the claim and earlier answers are rendered into the request (render_claim, escape): a change there, such as escaping ampersands in review 6, changes what the model receives without a new approval or making published results stale. Nor do they pin the tool definitions the model is offered (the MCP tools' descriptions in tools/server.py, sent through ToolGateway.definitions) or the reviewer-feedback instructions written by render_feedback: an edit to either changes what the model reads without a new approval. The owner is checked against the list of people when the registry loads, and the kill switch (HM-19) records the owner ID given for each stop; risk tier is an inventory field that nothing reads at run time.

### HM-04

**Routing to a person, decided in code** (built)

A code rule, not a model, decides whether a person must decide. It checks the facts behind the agents' labels: denials, payouts above $2,500 or above the amount claimed, any fraud flag or non-low risk, critic changes, unresolved critic disagreement and injection signals. Against the policy record it checks in code that the claimant is the insured (names compared ignoring case, accents and punctuation), the incident falls inside the coverage period and the claim is in USD; a claim with no policy record, no incident date or another currency goes to a person. Against the claim documents it checks that each claimed item matches its own occurrence of that figure, that the total claimed appears in them, and that the items add up to the total. Only figures the documents mark as US dollars count ($, US$ or USD on the same line beside them, with no other currency code or symbol and no minus sign touching the mark), and never part of a date, a time or a reference, so a year read as the claim, or a claim in euros or Canadian dollars the intake labels USD, goes to a person. Another currency counts beside the mark or after the figure: any three-capital code but USD, and a list of dollar and peso codes, prefixes and names in any case (HK $450, NZ $450, (CAD) $450, Cdn. $450, $450 cad, $450 in CAD, $450 Canadian), with any spaces (non-breaking ones too) or punctuation between. The list is a backstop behind the intake's label and the senior-adjuster rule for claims not in USD, not a currency parser.

### HM-05

**Adjuster approval gate** (built)

Claims routed to a person pause in a LangGraph workflow with state saved in SQLite. A decision must be signed with the adjuster's Ed25519 key over the claim, its fields and the evidence hash; come from a registered adjuster within their authority; quote the hash of the evidence pack the claim was paused with, which must match the pack stored under that hash in the audit database; on a USD claim pay no more than was claimed, and give a reason for any override or denial. Only senior adjusters may pay a claim with a fraud flag or above-low risk, or one the AI never assessed for fraud (a stopped claim); denying more than an adjuster's authority, or paying or denying a claim whose amount can't be compared with USD limits (not in USD, or nothing extracted), also needs a senior adjuster. The adjuster is shown the stored pack, with a warning if the checkpoint no longer matches it. A decision signed with a public demo key is marked as such in the record. A claim gets one evidence pack and one final decision: the audit log refuses a second of either inside the same write transaction, so a second run, two adjusters at once, or a checkpoints file restored from a backup can't have its result recorded or decide or pay the claim again. The log, not the checkpoint, says whether and how a claim was decided: a decision in the checkpoint that the log doesn't hold, or that differs from it, is reported as a problem. A claim whose evidence pack the log holds but whose checkpoint has no result is reported as needing a person. The review CLI refuses an invalid decision (a negative payout or NaN, say) with a message naming the fields.

### HM-06

**Tamper-evident audit log** (built)

Each claim's triage (agent calls, tool calls, critic reviews, revisions, corrections, result) and every adjuster decision are written, as they happen, to an append-only SQLite log in which each entry's hash covers the previous one. Triggers refuse edits and deletes, and verify reports any changed, missing or reordered entry, any stored evidence pack that no longer matches its hash, and (opened read-only, as the audit CLI does) any trigger someone removed. Entries hold no claim narrative or documents; the adjuster's written reason, critic issues, a rejected submission, each agent call's hash of what the agent was sent and each tool call's hash of its result are kept as keyed hashes (HMAC-SHA256), so the hashes alone can't confirm a guess (but see the gaps on signatures). Traces leave the input hash out. Every read and write takes the same lock.

### HM-19

**Kill switch** (built)

An agent's owner can stop it by adding an entry to registry/stops.yaml (python -m hall_monitor.kill_switch stop), which changes no manifest or approval and has no code owner. The registry reads the stop list on every check, so a running process refuses the agent on its next model call, including the next tool round of a call already under way, and on its next tool call; a stopped agent stops the claim and sends it to a person. Every agent a claim's result rests on is checked again just before routing, so a claim that used the answer of an agent stopped after it gave it also goes to a person. The stop list fails closed: if it is missing, unreadable or has a key twice, names an unknown agent or a person not in registry/owners.yaml, or approvals clear a stop it doesn't hold, every agent is refused. Restarting needs the stop cleared in registry/approvals.yaml (python -m hall_monitor.approvals --resume), a reviewed approval. CI fails a pull request that removes or edits a stop, or leaves a stop list that isn't one (the check says so). Refusals carry the stop and owner IDs, never the reason. Stops are written under a lock and replaced in one step, so two owners stopping agents at once both take effect; a symlinked stop list is written through the link. ### HM-21

**Leave eligibility and routing in code (HR example)** (partial)

The second worked example's governance core, before any agent exists: federal FMLA eligibility and entitlement are checked by ordinary code against synthetic employee and worksite records (python -m hall_monitor.hr). Employer coverage (a public agency, or 50 or more employees in 20 workweeks this or last calendar year), 52 weeks on the payroll (any week with a day of employment counts; service before a break of 7 years or more doesn't), 1,250 hours worked in the 12 months before leave, 50 employees within 75 surface miles of the worksite (the employer's own sites, inclusive), a qualifying reason, and whether the leave fits in the weeks left, counted in workdays of the employee's normal workweek, under each of the four leave-year methods (26 weeks for military caregiver leave, in one period measured forward from its first workday; while that period is open, other leave is held to the 12 weeks for other reasons and to the 26 less all leave in the period, whichever is less). A code rule then sends every check that didn't pass and every flag (intermittent leave, retaliation risk, a possible accommodation, a state with its own leave law where the employee works or lives, a key employee, a spouse at the same employer where leave may be shared, bonding leave running past its 12 months) to HR review, and names the least senior role that may decide: denying or finding an employee ineligible needs an HR manager or above, extending past the entitlement or deciding a case with a retaliation risk needs an HR director. Even a clean request is a draft for a leave specialist to sign, never an approval without a person. The records hold no names and no diagnosis; the health information they do hold is coarse (the reason category of a request and of leave already taken, whether an adult child can't care for themselves, signs of an accommodation need), and bonding requests carry the date of the birth or placement. A test fixes the list of fields of every record, nested ones included, so a new one is a reviewed change. 28 synthetic requests sit on the boundaries, each with an answer key worked out by hand.

### HM-22

**Leave agents inside the shared controls (HR example)** (partial)

Two agents prepare a leave request for a person, through the same call path, registry, approvals, kill switch, data-class check and audit log as the claims agents (hall_monitor/hr/pipeline.py). The leave intake agent reads the employee's message after identifier redaction and returns dates, a reason category and yes-or-no facts only: its answer has no free-text field, so a diagnosis has nowhere to go. The rules in code (HM-21) then decide eligibility, entitlement, the route and the role. The intake agent's answer is what the rules run on, so a wrong extraction changes the result; the eval measures that. The leave explanation agent reads only the code's result, never the message, the employee ID or the request ID, and writes an advisory note for the specialist (a list of one note per check), kept only if it covers exactly the checks the code made; it can't change the route or the role. Text in the message aimed at the AI (the injection detector, run on the message as sent and as redacted) sends the request to HR review whatever the agents say. Any failure of intake or the rules stops the request and sends it to a person, keeping the code's reasons if it had routed (a failure of the intake call itself leaves no evidence pack, so the request is listed as stopped and can be prepared again once the cause is fixed): a refusal, a stopped or unapproved intake agent, an API error, any unexpected error, an extraction without dates, with an end before its start or with a date outside 1900 to 2199, no employee record, or a cost that can't be shown. A failed or stopped explanation agent doesn't stop the request: it goes to the same person without a note, the log says why, and the eval counts it. Each agent's input is declared with the data classes it carries and checked against its hall pass before the model is called: personal and health data for intake, health only (the reason category) for the explanation, whose input also holds the employee's employment facts (hours, weeks of service, worksite), which no data class covers. Each step is written to the audit log as it happens: IDs, keyed hashes (HMAC), counts, codes and costs, never the message, the extraction's values or the explanation's text. The only plain hashes are of the published prompt templates: each agent call's hash of what the agent was sent is logged keyed, and kept out of traces, because the explanation's input has so few possible values that a plain hash could be confirmed by guessing. An eval (python -m hall_monitor.hr.evals) scores intake field by field and the end-to-end result against the answer keys, including the intake errors the rules can't see.

### HM-23

**Signed HR sign-off within the role the rules name (HR example)** (built)

A person decides each leave request that is prepared into the audit log (python -m hall_monitor.hr.run, which calls the API, then python -m hall_monitor.hr.review). Each prepared request leaves an evidence pack in the audit database, stored once under its hash (the redacted message, the structured request, the code's checks and route, the explanation, and a random nonce so the hash can't be confirmed by guessing); preparing a request again is refused, and the chain check reports a pack that was changed or erased. A decision counts only if the staff member is in registry/hr_staff.yaml, it is signed with their Ed25519 key over the request, every decision field and the evidence hash, and code finds it within their role: at least the role the routing rule named; approving FMLA leave only for an eligible employee with a qualifying reason, for more than 0 weeks and no more than are left, were asked or qualify (bonding weeks after the 12 months don't); approving fewer weeks than asked (for intermittent leave, than are left) only by an HR manager or above, with a reason, since it refuses the rest; denying only by an HR manager or above, with a reason; extending only by an HR director, with a reason, for an eligible employee whose request is past the entitlement, granting more weeks in total than are left and no more than were asked; approving a request the rule sent to HR review only with a reason. Asking for more information is open to anyone registered and isn't a decision: it is signed, stored and logged once, and the request keeps waiting. The audit log decides whether a request was decided: one final decision per request, checked inside the write. Every refusal that reaches these rules, for a request in the log, is logged with why, including one that lost a race or met a changed or erased pack, and names the staff member only if they signed it; the CLI's refusals of malformed input (weeks out of range, an unreadable key, a bad staff file) aren't decisions and aren't logged. The log keeps the staff ID and role, the outcome and weeks, the code's route, and keyed hashes of the reason and the signed decision; the signed decision, with its reason and signature, is stored before the log entry is written (so a logged decision always has it, and one that lost a race is removed) beside the evidence pack in the store that can be erased, not in the chain, so no signature in the chain can confirm a guessed reason. What HR is shown comes from the pack, so a run that lost the race to prepare a request can't change it.

### HM-25

### HM-26

**Complaint ingestion from CFPB** (built)

The complaint desk reads public CFPB complaints through one MCP tool, cfpb_complaint_search, behind the tool gateway: only the pipeline may call it, its arguments are checked (a date, a digit cursor, a page size up to 100) and every call is in the audit log. Under it, CfpbClient (egress.py) makes GET requests through the egress guard (HM-25), spaced one a second by default, retrying timeouts, network errors, 429 and 5xx with exponential backoff, jitter and Retry-After up to a cap; any other failure stops the pull, which records failed with the error's type and keeps what it stored. The tool keeps an allow-list of fields, so the company name never enters the system and new fields are dropped and named; ZIP codes are cut to three digits. Pulls take the desk's products, oldest first, resume after a stored cursor (CFPB pages by search_after) that moves only after a page is processed, and stop at the desk's capacity, saying whether they caught up. Records are validated strictly and kept by complaint ID: a repeat is skipped, a change is a new version, a failure is quarantined once with field names and error types only. Each page is written to disk with a manifest of hashes before it is processed; a snapshot replays offline, a changed one is refused, and the pinned snapshot under data/cfpb/snapshots/ (its manifest hash recorded in code) is what evals and demos run on. CFPB's metadata reaches the audit log only in the expected shape (a timestamp, booleans, a count) and unknown field names only as a placeholder; a page that doesn't move the cursor forward fails the pull. Pulls log JSON lines and one complaint_pull audit entry with counts, cursor, retries and status.

### HM-27

**Complaint triage, routed in code** (built)

Every complaint goes to a person; nothing is resolved automatically. A code rule (complaints/routing.py) reads CFPB's own fields and sends to specialist review, at high urgency, any complaint whose product, issue or sub-issue names fraud, a scam, or a transaction or charge the consumer didn't authorise or make, identity theft (including an account, loan, debt or credit inquiry the consumer says isn't theirs), foreclosure or a mortgage the consumer is struggling to pay, or discrimination, and any tagged Older American or Servicemember; an untimely response, text aimed at the AI, an agent that failed (refused, stopped, unapproved, an API error, an answer that didn't validate), an agent whose confidence was low or that couldn't tell the category, an agent urgency below the code's floor, and a concern the agent raised that the code didn't, go there too. The rest go to the standard queue, still for a person. The complaint triage agent (Haiku, no tools, approved for PII and financial data) reads the structured fields and the redacted free text, fenced and escaped as untrusted, without the complaint ID, state or ZIP prefix, and returns a summary, category, urgency, confidence, concerns and a draft acknowledgement. It can add reasons and raise urgency, never remove or lower them; a failure never invents an answer. The draft is kept only if it passes a pattern check (no refund, credit, waiver, guarantee, "we will" commitment or deadline; no link, bare domain, email address or phone number), and is never sent (HM-25). The audit log, scoped cfpb-<complaint id>, records the complaint's hash, redaction counts and injection signals, each agent call (input hash keyed), and the route with its reasons, urgencies, category, a keyed hash of the summary, the draft's hash and the cost, and whether the answer came from the scripted stand-in; never the summary or draft text. A complaint is triaged once: its received entry is written once, before the model is called, so a second worker stops with no model call. The label patterns are checked against every label in the pinned snapshot by a hand-written table in the tests.
