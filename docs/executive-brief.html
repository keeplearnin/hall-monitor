AI that prepares insurance claims, with people making the calls that matter

hall-monitor shows how a regulated firm can use AI agents to speed up claims work and still answer the regulator's questions: what each AI was allowed to do, who approved it, what it touched, and who made the final decision. The rules live in software, not in a policy binder.

What a regulator will ask

  • What is this AI allowed to do? BuiltIts hall pass lists the model, data and tools. Anything without a pass is refused.
  • Who is accountable? BuiltEvery pass names an owner and a risk level.
  • Who makes the big calls? PartlyDenials, large payouts and fraud go to a person. The adjuster's approval screen is next.
  • Is it accurate? BuiltScored against answer keys and published, misses included.
  • Can you prove it months later? PartlyEach step records its pass fingerprint. A tamper-evident log is planned.

Roadmap

  1. 1-2Hall passes, four AI steps, first published scores
  2. 3A checker that double-checks the AI's work before it moves on
  3. 4Controlled access to policy and claims history
  4. 5Adjuster approval screen that saves the evidence they saw
  5. 6Tamper-evident record of every step
  6. 7Cheaper models for simple claims, cost tracking
  7. 8Privacy masking, fairness checks, attack tests
  8. 9-10Mapped to NIST AI RMF and FINOS; quality gate on every change; external review
A reference implementation, not a production claims system. All claims, policies and people are fictional. Scores: 6 test claims × 3 runs, November 2026. A small sample: it shows the kinds of mistakes, not reliable rates. github.com/keeplearnin/hall-monitor
