HALL PASShall-monitor · briefing for business leaders · November 2026 · after milestone 11 and Phase B so far

AI that prepares insurance claims, with people making the calls that matter

hall-monitor shows how a regulated firm can use AI agents to speed up claims work and still answer the regulator's questions: what each AI was allowed to do, who approved it, what records it looked at, and who made the final decision. The rules live in software, not in a policy binder.

What a regulator will ask

  • What is this AI allowed to do? BuiltIts permit lists the model, the records and the lookups it may use, for this claim only. Anything else is refused and logged.
  • Who approved it? BuiltEvery permit names an owner, and each version and its core instructions need a committed approval to run (the policy wording, look-up descriptions and correction text aren't covered yet). Who may approve is set by repository controls, not yet enforced.
  • Who makes the big calls? BuiltDenials, large payouts, fraud, and a decision the checking AI changed or still disputes wait for an adjuster, within their authority limit.
  • What happens to personal data? BuiltID numbers are removed from claim text before the AI sees it; no claim narrative goes into logs or traces.
  • Is it accurate and fair? PartlyScored on 34 test claims, on earlier versions, misses published. Fairness and attack tests are built; their results are next.
  • Can you prove it months later? BuiltA hash-chained record shows any change. Keep a copy of its latest hash elsewhere to catch a full rewrite.

Roadmap

  1. 1-3Permits, five AI steps including a double-check, published scores
  2. 4-5Approved data access; adjuster approval tied to the evidence they were shown
  3. 6Tamper-evident record of each claim's AI steps and decision
  4. 7Cost per claim; cheaper models for small claims (off until tested)
  5. 8Privacy, attack defences, fairness and attack tests (results next)
  6. 9Mapped to NIST AI RMF and FINOS; quality gate on every change
  7. 10Decision records, board brief, review package (outside reviewers invited)
  8. 11Hardening: signed adjuster decisions, evidence checked before a decision, data limits enforced at run time
  9. 12Phase B so far: tests of every AI step giving wrong answers, an owner's stop switch, more ID numbers removed (not yet scored live)
  10. 13Test harness for vendor guardrails, gateways and observability; limits as policy code
  11. 25A supervisor AI now picks the order of the steps for claims; code checks every step, holds a budget per claim and alone decides the outcome (tested offline only; the scores above are from the older fixed order)
A reference implementation, not a production claims system. All claims, policies and people are fictional. Scores: 34 test claims × 3 runs plus a planted-mistake test, November 2026; they predate milestones 4-11 and Phase B (including stricter routing checks and the kill switch), whose live scores are next.
